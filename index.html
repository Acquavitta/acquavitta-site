The science

Fifty years of peptides moving from the lab bench to the bathroom shelf

Signal and carrier peptides didn't start in skincare — they started as tools for studying wound repair. Here is the (brief, honest) timeline of how they got here.

1973

GHK is isolated

The tripeptide glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine is identified in human plasma while researchers study why older plasma affects liver cell regeneration differently than younger plasma.

1980s–90s

Copper complexes enter wound research

GHK's copper-binding form, copper tripeptide-1, is studied for its role in tissue repair signaling, laying groundwork for its later use in topical formulation.

1990s

Matrixyl is patented

Palmitoyl pentapeptide formulations are developed and patented as a cosmetic ingredient, designed to be stable and skin-penetrating at usable concentrations.

2000s

Neuropeptides join the category

Short-chain peptides modeled on neurotransmitter-blocking mechanisms, like acetyl hexapeptide-8, are formulated as topical alternatives aimed at expression lines.

Today

Formulation, not novelty, is the constraint

The limiting factor isn't finding new peptides — it's stabilizing them, keeping them bioavailable, and being transparent about what's actually in the bottle. That's the part AcquaVitta is built around.

Why peptides

Small chains, specific jobs

Peptides are short chains of amino acids — fragments of the proteins your skin already makes. Because they're small, well-defined molecules, they can be formulated at a known concentration and studied for a specific, narrow job: signaling collagen production, relaxing surface tension, or supporting the skin barrier.

  • Signal peptides — tell skin cells to behave as they did earlier in life, prompting collagen and elastin synthesis.
  • Carrier peptides — deliver trace minerals like copper directly to where enzymatic repair processes need them.
  • Neurotransmitter-inhibiting peptides — soften the look of expression lines by limiting the muscle signal that forms them.

We publish the peptide, its concentration, and the study category behind it on every product page — no proprietary blends.
